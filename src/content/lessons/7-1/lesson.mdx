## About this practice exam

This exam has **15 questions** drawn from three of the six scenarios: the **customer support resolution agent**, the **multi-agent research system** and **developer productivity tools**. Together they concentrate on **Domain 1** (Agentic Architecture & Orchestration), **Domain 2** (Tool Design & MCP Integration) and **Domain 5** (Context Management & Reliability), which make up 60% of the real exam's weight.

The questions are new. None are taken from the real exam or copied from the exam guide's samples, but they're written to the same pattern: a production context, a symptom, a qualifier such as "most effective" or "first step", and four or five plausible options. The quiz scores it like the exam, with all-or-nothing multi-select, and you pass with **12 of 15**.

## How to take it

1. **Time yourself.** The real exam gives you 120 minutes for 60 items, about 2 minutes each. Give yourself **30 minutes** here, and don't look anything up.
2. **Answer every question before submitting.** On the exam, an unanswered item is simply wrong, so an educated guess is always better.
3. **Write down your confidence** for each answer (sure, leaning, guessing) before you submit. Afterward, compare it with your results. Confident misses tell you more than lucky guesses do.

## A method for scenario questions

Most items can be worked through in four steps:

1. **Find the qualifier.** "Most effective", "first step", "most maintainable" and "guarantee" each point to a different kind of answer. "Guarantee" usually means a deterministic control. "First step" usually means the least-effort fix that addresses the root cause.
2. **Name the root cause** in a few words before you read the options: "the coordinator's decomposition", "vague tool descriptions", "context passed as prose".
3. **Eliminate by pattern.** Strike options that rely on prompt wording when a guarantee is needed, add infrastructure before trying the simple fix, treat a symptom downstream of the cause, or use features that don't exist.
4. **Choose between the last two** by asking which one fixes the cause you named, not which one sounds most thorough.

For multi-select items, judge each option on its own. Two correct answers often fix two different parts of the problem.

<KeyTakeaways>

- Work each item as **qualifier → root cause → eliminate by pattern → choose**.
- **Guarantees** come from code (hooks, gates, schemas). **Selection problems** usually start with descriptions and scope. **Context problems** come from organization, not window size.
- Track **confidence as well as correctness**. Confident misses show where your understanding needs work.

</KeyTakeaways>
